# Honey Print Shop — Brand Guidelines & AI Agent Brief

> **Positioning:** Premium-feeling, approachable apparel — *Where stories meet adventure.*  
> **Core territories:** Books • Travel • Intentional Living  
> **Brand in five words:** Thoughtful • Crafted • Warm • Understated • Curious

## 1. Brand at a glance
Honey Print Shop is a thoughtful, design-led print-on-demand apparel brand for people who love books, travel, and intentional living. It brings stories and adventure together through wearable designs that feel considered, expressive, and easy to live in.

The shop is a small husband-and-wife business. The brand should feel personal and carefully made, without pretending products are handmade when they are professionally printed and fulfilled by a print-on-demand partner.

## 2. Audience
- Primary: readers and book lovers, especially women, who enjoy literary culture, cozy rituals, and expressive but tasteful apparel.
- Adjacent: travelers, curious explorers, and people drawn to intentional everyday living.
- They value quality over fast fashion, good design, comfort, meaningful details, and products that express an interest without feeling loud or gimmicky.
- They appreciate a boutique feel: distinctive and considered, not mass-market novelty merchandise.

Write to an intelligent, discerning customer. Do not talk down to them or assume every customer has the same age, lifestyle, or identity.

## 3. Brand voice
**Warm, never gushy. Thoughtful, never pretentious. Cozy, never cutesy. Adventurous, never rugged. Premium, never exclusive.**

- Use clear, natural language and a measured amount of personality.
- Favor specific imagery and meaningful details over generic hype.
- Keep sentences concise. Let a good phrase breathe.
- Use gentle wit when it fits, especially for bookish or lifestyle humor.
- Use emojis sparingly; avoid excessive exclamation points, ALL CAPS, slang-heavy copy, and hard-sell urgency.

**Lean into:** stories, pages, chapters, curiosity, discovery, wander, collect moments, slow mornings, well-loved books, thoughtful details, everyday adventure, cozy layers.

**Avoid:** “obsessed,” “must-have,” “the perfect everything,” “girlie,” “boss babe,” “live laugh love,” forced puns, generic empowerment slogans, aggressive sales language, exaggerated luxury/craftsmanship claims.

## 4. Visual identity and art direction
**Overall aesthetic:** modern boutique apparel with a soft literary sensibility and understated alpine/outdoors influence. Editorial, tactile, quietly distinctive, timeless—not trendy. Reference worlds: bookshops, stationery, vintage field guides, national-park ephemera, artisan printmaking, calm travel journals.

**Principles**
- Understated first: intentional and wearable, not overloaded.
- Tactile and crafted: subtle letterpress, woodblock, paper grain, ink texture.
- Editorial composition: thoughtful spacing, hierarchy, negative space, restrained ornament.
- Soft adventure: mountains, stars, compass motifs, trees, maps; reflective, not extreme-sports.
- Feminine-neutral: gentle and warm, not overtly girly, floral-heavy, or pastel-cute.
- One clear idea is usually stronger than a collage of symbols.

**Palette direction:** warm, natural, muted colors—cream, parchment, oatmeal, warm gray, charcoal, forest/sage greens, muted blue, dusty/mineral tones, restrained gold/tan. Reference colors explored: `#3A4D42`, `#4F6A5C`, `#C49A5A`, `#D4AF7A`. These are references, not mandatory colors for every product.

**Typography:** literary/editorial serif typography; TAN Aegean and EB Garamond have been explored. Pair expressive type with restrained, readable supporting type. Avoid novelty fonts and generic craft-market clip-art styling. Prioritize print-size and thumbnail legibility.

**Motifs:** books, pages, type, subtle stars/constellations, compass marks, mountain silhouettes, pine trees, trails, maps, travel ephemera, restrained botanical details. Utah/local cues should be subtle—alpine, native-flower, or apple-blossom—not souvenir-like.

**Important:** a small honey detail may be a quiet signature, but do not make bees, beehives, honey pots, or Utah beehive symbolism a central theme. The brand name does not require honey imagery.

**Avoid visually:** juvenile illustration, loud Western/cowboy styling, rugged tactical/outdoors aesthetics, busy compositions, generic Canva-looking layouts, excessive distress, random icons, heavy bee/hive imagery, and imitation of another brand or artist.

## 5. Product and design direction
- Core territories: **BOOKS, TRAVEL, INTENTIONAL LIVING**.
- Bookish: literary, cozy, witty, romantic, or quietly book-obsessed, but wearable beyond a fleeting trend.
- Travel: discovery, place, and the joy of going somewhere. Do not default to hiking/camping unless the concept calls for it.
- Lifestyle: thoughtful and relatable, with a point of view rather than generic inspiration.
- Type 1 diabetes designs are a distinct collection. Humor can be candid and community-aware; do not trivialize medical risk or make medical claims.
- Designs must be readable, balanced, printable, and attractive at garment scale. Consider garment color, contrast, placement, and artwork dimensions.
- Do not force every design to include the logo or honey imagery.

## 6. Product quality and fulfillment language
Honey Print Shop uses print-on-demand production and fulfillment. Be accurate and transparent.
- Use exact blank/style and verified supplier details for each listing.
- Never invent fabric weights, fiber percentages, sustainability claims, certifications, production locations, or shipping timelines.
- Do not say handmade, hand-sewn, locally printed, or made in-house unless verified.
- Say “professionally printed” only when accurate; do not misstate the print method.
- Fit guidance must match the specific blank.
- Care instructions must match the product/provider. A previously used template is wash cold inside out, tumble dry low, and do not iron directly on the design—but verify each product.

## 7. Etsy listing standards
**Recommended structure**
1. Short appealing opening connecting the design to the customer's interest or feeling.
2. Accurate garment and design details, including blank brand/style when known.
3. Fit and sizing guidance, including how to choose an oversized fit.
4. Verified care instructions.
5. A tailored **Perfect for** section when useful.
6. Warm, low-pressure close. No fake scarcity or pushy CTA.

**SEO and tags**
- Provide 13 tags when requested.
- Use relevant buyer-intent phrases and varied terms.
- Check Etsy's current tag character limit.
- Mix product type, audience, theme, occasion, style, and gifting terms.
- Avoid near-duplicate tags and awkward keyword stuffing.
- Never claim tags are high-volume or guaranteed to rank without current evidence.

## 8. Social and marketing
- Feel like a thoughtful note from a small creative shop, not a constant sales pitch.
- Mix product features with bookish observations, travel inspiration, behind-the-scenes context, seasonal moments, and customer-use scenarios.
- One clear idea per post; conversational opening.
- Make product mentions organic and explain what makes the design meaningful, useful, or giftable.
- Avoid generic engagement bait, repetitive “shop now,” and manufactured urgency.
- Use specific hashtags; avoid unrelated broad hashtag blocks.
- Verify book/author/song/pop-culture references. Do not reproduce copyrighted lyrics or substantial copyrighted text.

## 9. Photography and mockups
- Natural, believable lifestyle imagery with an editorial boutique feel.
- Casual iPhone-photo quality can work for social: natural Utah light, real settings, relaxed composition—not glossy studio perfection.
- Flatlay props can include books, plain notebooks/travel journals, coffee or hot chocolate, folded blankets, simple stationery, or a subtle compass.
- Props should not compete with the garment. Avoid third-party logos and readable book covers/titles unless intentional.
- Garment and print are the hero. Show realistic folds, sleeves, scale, and print placement.
- Clean neutral backgrounds aid clarity; themed settings should support—not overwhelm—the product.
- Never misrepresent garment color, fit, print size, or product details.

## 10. AI agent instructions (copy into an agent's system/project prompt)
You are creating work for Honey Print Shop. Treat this guide as the source of truth. Create thoughtful, design-led apparel content for a boutique print-on-demand brand centered on books, travel, and intentional living. The brand voice is warm, intelligent, understated, cozy-not-cute, adventurous-not-rugged, and premium-but-approachable. Favor clear, natural writing, specific details, restrained humor, and editorial simplicity. Avoid hype, hard selling, clichés, excessive emojis, juvenile visuals, loud Western styling, generic inspirational language, and literal bee/beehive branding. The brand name does not mean every design needs bees, honey, or hive imagery.

Before producing work, identify the product category, target customer, purpose, format, and supplied product facts. Preserve exact design wording. Do not invent garment specifications, materials, fit, production details, shipping times, care instructions, medical claims, or SEO performance. If a required fact is missing, flag it or ask rather than guessing. For Etsy listings, use a compelling concise opening, accurate product details, fit, verified care, a relevant Perfect For section when appropriate, and 13 distinct relevant tags when requested. For social content, sound like a thoughtful small creative shop rather than an ad. For visual concepts, prioritize restrained composition, tactile printmaking/editorial references, muted natural colors, legibility, and realistic garment presentation. Keep travel concepts about discovery and place rather than automatically turning them into hiking/camping themes. Treat Type 1 diabetes designs with candid, community-aware humor and respect; never trivialize medical risk or make health claims. Do not imitate other artists or brands. When uncertain, choose the simpler, more considered option and explain assumptions.

**Required quality check**
- Does this feel thoughtful, warm, and considered—not generic or overly cute?
- Does it fit a core territory, or is there a clear reason it belongs?
- Is it readable, useful, and appropriate for the platform?
- Are product, fulfillment, sizing, care, and health claims verified?
- Have clichés, unnecessary honey/bee imagery, loud sales language, and clutter been removed?
- Would the intended customer genuinely want to wear, read, save, or share this?

## 11. Examples
**On-brand**
- “For the chapters you stay up late for.”
- “A little more room for the next adventure.”
- “A cozy layer for slow mornings and one-more-chapter nights.”
- Mountain-and-constellation graphic with generous negative space, muted ink, and a small compass detail.

**Off-brand**
- “OMG YOU NEED THIS!!! The CUTEST hoodie EVER!!!”
- Cartoon bee, honeycomb background, and hive puns solely because the shop is named Honey.
- Generic camping icons for a design about city travel or cultural discovery.
- Calling a POD sweatshirt handmade or inventing fabric composition.

## 12. Established vs. to confirm
**Established:** books / travel / intentional living; thoughtful and crafted-care voice; premium yet approachable; understated alpine and literary influences; no overt bee/beehive theme; POD fulfillment.

**Explored references, not rigid rules:** TAN Aegean and EB Garamond; green/gold colors above; hexagon and subtle apple-blossom cues in identity exploration.

**Verify before standardizing:** final logo files and usage rules, official color values, font licenses, exact garment catalog/blanks, supplier-specific care instructions, shipping/returns policies, and current Etsy SEO limits.

Update this guide when brand decisions change so human collaborators and AI agents use one consistent source of truth.
